Marine PlayGround

easynav_playground_marine simulates marine vehicles in a synthetic sea: a surface boat, the WAM-V catamaran, and a submarine, the MBARI Tethys LRAUV. Each one carries the sensors of its kind. EasyNav drives the submarine: it plans 3D paths under the surface and over the sea floor on a Bonxai map, localizes it by dead reckoning as an AUV does, and follows the paths with MPC.

See Installation to install it. The vehicles are downloaded from Gazebo Fuel the first time (internet).

The world

The synthetic sea is generated offline, the same every time: 1 km x 1 km, with z = 0 at sea level. It has a beach to the west and a floor that deepens to the east (21 m at the origin, 48 m at the edge), an islet, a shoal 4 m under the surface, a seamount and a ridge. Optionally, waves move the vehicle and are drawn on the water (waves:=true).

Navigating with the submarine

ros2 launch easynav_playground_marine easynav_submarine_mpc.launch.yaml

It starts Gazebo, the Tethys at 3 m deep, EasyNav and RViz2. RViz2 shows the Bonxai map of the sea floor, the planned path, the MPC’s prediction, the estimated and the true pose, the goal and the sensors: the imaging sonar (its fan image and its echoes), the DVL and the depth gauge.

Goals under the water

Goals go under the surface: use the 3D Goal Pose tool of navmap_rviz_plugin (shortcut h, see Aerial PlayGround for how it works), already in the toolbar of this PlayGround’s RViz2 configuration, or publish them:

ros2 topic pub --once /goal_pose geometry_msgs/msg/PoseStamped \
  "{header: {frame_id: map}, pose: {position: {x: 60.0, y: 0.0, z: -6.0}, orientation: {w: 1.0}}}"

Configuration

params/submarine_mpc.params.yaml:

  • Localization: FusionLocalizer, dead reckoning. The local filter (odom -> base_footprint) fuses the IMU and the DVL’s velocity over the floor; the global one (map -> odom) adds the depth gauge and the GNSS fix, which is only available at the surface. Both start where the launcher puts the vehicle, (0, 0) at 3 m deep.

  • Map: the Bonxai map of the sea floor (maps/synthetic_sea.pcd in easynav_playground_marine_worlds, a point every 2 m), at 1 m.

  • Planner: easynav_bonxai_planner with 2 m cells. The floor is solid (fill_below: lowest), the vehicle stays under the surface (max_altitude: -1) and at least 2 m from the relief, and prefers 5 m.

  • Controller: MPC with motion_model: aerial, forward only and with no lateral motion, within the Tethys’ speed (1.5 m/s), vertical speed and yaw rate (0.05 rad/s), with the path’s corners rounded to its turning circle.

The Tethys is a torpedo: it turns and dives only while it moves forward, with a turning radius of some 30 m. When the MPC asks it to turn at a standstill, to face a goal behind it, its autopilot does the turn on the move. With no thrust at the goal, it coasts on some 10-30 m.

The launcher accepts params_file, gui (false runs Gazebo headless), rviz (false does not start RViz2) and waves.

Sensors

The place, rate and noise of the sensors are in one table (tools/marine_sensors.py in easynav_playground_marine_worlds), from which the launcher makes the SDF, the bridge and the static transforms.

Vehicle

Sensors (topics)

Boat (WAM-V)

IMU (/imu/data), magnetometer (/imu/mag), GNSS (/gps/fix), 3D lidar on a mast (/lidar/points, only what is above the water), front camera (/camera/image_raw), echosounder (/echosounder)

Submarine (Tethys)

IMU (/imu/data), magnetometer (/imu/mag), depth gauge (/pressure, /depth), DVL (/dvl/data, /dvl/twist, /dvl/altitude), GNSS (/gps/fix, a fix only at the surface), forward-looking imaging sonar (/sonar/image, /sonar/image_fan, /sonar/points)

Gazebo has no acoustic sensor: the imaging sonar is a fan of rays whose hits a node turns into the echoes of a multibeam sonar (backscatter, beam pattern, absorption, shadows, speckle). It has the look and the geometry of a sonar image, not a physical simulation of sound. The true pose of the vehicle is /ground_truth/odom.

Simulation only

To drive the vehicles by hand, with Gazebo and RViz2 showing their sensors but without EasyNav:

ros2 launch easynav_playground_marine easynav_marine.launch.yaml robot:=boat
ros2 launch easynav_playground_marine easynav_marine.launch.yaml robot:=submarine

Both listen on /cmd_vel: linear.x forward, angular.z yaw rate and, for the submarine, linear.z up. A command older than a second stops the engines.

ros2 topic pub -r 10 /cmd_vel geometry_msgs/msg/Twist "{linear: {x: 1.5, z: -0.2}, angular: {z: 0.1}}"

Other arguments are altitude (the submarine’s depth), radius (the size of the sea), waves and its wave_height, wave_period and wave_direction. The README of the PlayGround explains how the waves move each vehicle.
